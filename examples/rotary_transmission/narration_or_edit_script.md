# Caption-driven edit script

This film uses typography and sound design rather than synthetic narration.

The evaluator starts empty. It owns the motor, both D connectors, the brake, the bearings, and the fixture—but none of the submitted mechanism.

The contract is explicit: six-millimetre D interfaces, ten millimetres of engagement, a thirty-five-newton insertion limit, speed control under a strict quarter-newton-metre torque cap, and an evaluator-owned brake. Metadata locates the equipment. Geometry must carry the load.

Each STEP solid remains a separate tetrahedral deformable body. The model is an effective isotropic printed-PLA approximation: stable Neo-Hookean recoverable elasticity, mass from density, and frictional IPC contact. It does not predict yielding, fracture, fatigue, heat, or layer separation.

Every substantive candidate stays in the ledger. Initial overlaps are rejected. Numerical failure is inconclusive—not a jam. Motion, ratio, strain, wobble, slip, work, and solver status come from the simulation state.

The final sequence may say PASS only when physical insertion, contact-only transmission, brake load, acceptance thresholds, solver convergence, policy audit, and deterministic repeatability all agree.

The evaluator never trusted the design. It tested the geometry.

Onscreen disclaimer: “Simulation engineering evaluation under the shown model and settings; not certification of real-world safety.”
